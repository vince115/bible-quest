# Boss encounter design

## Building blocks
- **Telegraph / intent** — show what the boss will do next, including the target. Creates the defensive choice.
- **Phases** — the fight changes shape at a milestone; each phase asks a different question.
- **Armor / immunity** — a lock that a specific action opens. Must *reduce*, not nullify, unless the lock
  phase is short and the key is clearly progressing.
- **Enrage** — the boss gets more dangerous after the milestone, creating urgency.
- **Counterplay** — every threat has at least one readable answer (shield, fear removal, heal, kill first).
- **Setup → payoff** — the player prepares, then lands a decisive blow.
- **Readability** — the player can always answer: what is it doing, why, and what can I do about it?

## Goliath's intended arc
```text
Fear → Preparation → Faith → Break Armor → Power spike → Victory
```
| Beat | The player should feel | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Fear | Pressure; the giant is terrifying | Fear that only feels like bookkeeping |
| Preparation | "I'm getting ready" — visible progress | Turns where nothing visibly changes |
| Faith | Anticipation as the meter fills | Faith filling with no choice involved |
| Break Armor | A climax: Sling Stone lands | Damage that's barely bigger than a normal hit |
| Power spike | The team can finally hurt him | A long finisher grind |
| Victory | Earned, decisive | A win that never felt at risk |

## Checks
- Is the armor phase short or still showing progress? A hard cap that turns every hit into 1 kills the arc.
- Can Goliath actually threaten a character within a 3–5 turn fight? If not, defence decisions are fake.
- Is every telegraphed threat answerable with cards or skills the player is likely to have?
- Never change boss rules without the user's approval; propose with numbers and simulation.
