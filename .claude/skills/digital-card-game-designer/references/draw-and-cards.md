# Draw design

## The variables

| Variable | What it controls | Typical tension |
|---|---|---|
| Deck size | How predictable draws are; how often a card returns | Small = consistent but stale; large = surprising but swingy |
| Opening hand | First-turn options | Too small = no choice; too big = no anticipation |
| Draw per turn | Flow of new options | Too low = stuck; too high = hand floods, cards feel disposable |
| Hand limit | Whether holding is a decision | No limit = hoarding; tight limit = forced plays/discards |
| Discard / reshuffle | Recursion speed | Fast cycling makes every card feel common |
| Retention | Do unplayed cards stay? | Slay the Spire discards the hand each turn (use-it-or-lose-it); TCGs keep it (planning) |
| Selection | Scry, choose-1-of-N, mulligan | Smooths bad luck, adds micro-decisions |

## Why a new card creates anticipation
1. **Known pool, unknown order** — you know the outs you're hoping for.
2. **The draw can change this turn's plan** — it arrives before you commit.
3. **Not every draw is equal** — some draws are "the one I needed".
4. **The draw is a moment** — it is shown, named and animated, not silently appended.
If the hand is usually full, or most cards are interchangeable, or the deck is exhausted early, the draw
stops being a moment.

## Dead cards
A dead card is one you can't or wouldn't play now. A few create good "hold or wait" decisions; many create
frustration. Watch for cards that are dead for a whole phase (e.g. damage cards while a boss is immune).

## Card advantage in a single PvE encounter
Without an opponent's hand, card advantage means **options available per turn** and **wasted draws**.
Measure: cards played per turn, cards left in hand, draws lost to the hand limit, distinct cards seen by
the expected final turn.

## Draw checklist for Bible Quest
- [ ] Each turn, does the player *see* what they drew (animation, "new" marker)?
- [ ] Does the draw arrive before decisions, so it can change the plan?
- [ ] Is the hand limit causing silently wasted draws? (Show it or change it.)
- [ ] By the expected last turn (4), how much of the deck has been seen? (Most of it = little anticipation.)
- [ ] Are cards played most turns, or crowded out by other actions?
- [ ] Is there at least one "hold this for the right moment" card in a typical hand?
- [ ] Is any card dead for an entire phase? Is that intentional?
- [ ] Can the player tell what's left in the deck and what's in the discard?
